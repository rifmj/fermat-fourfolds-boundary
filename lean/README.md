# Lean formalisation — _The Hodge conjecture for Fermat fourfolds of odd degree at most 199_

Machine-checks the manuscript's combinatorial/arithmetic core and its deduction chains, in the
same two-tier style as the surface companion's `repo/lean/` (arXiv:2607.17387), plus a new
middle tier — a **claim calculus** — that certifies the citation chains themselves, a
**full-census tier** (a provably complete enumerator swept over EVERY odd level 3..199 and
the probe levels 231/273/297), and the **Theorem-B legs** (codimension 0/1/3/4 and the
h²-summand reduction) as a machine-checked derivation from nine cited fields.

## Build

```
lake exe cache get     # once: prebuilt mathlib oleans (only Forms needs them)
lake build             # all thirteen libraries
```

The census sweeps are the expensive part (heavy `native_decide`: the meet-in-the-middle
enumeration, canonization and closure sweep run per level); lake builds the five sweep
batches in parallel.  Reference timings (Apple M4 Max): Sweeps5 (297) 281 s, Sweeps4
(231+273) 315 s, Sweeps2 (151–179) 629 s, Sweeps3 (181–199) 687 s, and Sweeps1 (the 74
levels 3–149 in one evaluation, plus the cross-engine check) ≈ 5.5 h — the closure scans
on the quasi-rich mid-size composite levels dominate; splitting batch 1 further is a known
easy optimisation, deliberately left simple.

Tier 1/2/1.5/2.5 also compile with a bare toolchain (no lake, no mathlib; `Std` is part of
the toolchain):

```
lean BoundaryData.lean -o BoundaryData.olean
LEAN_PATH=. lean BoundaryCore.lean        # ~30 s: the census enumerations run here
LEAN_PATH=. lean BoundaryClaim.lean
LEAN_PATH=. lean BoundaryValidity.lean
LEAN_PATH=. lean BoundaryCensus.lean
LEAN_PATH=. lean BoundaryCensusSweeps1.lean   # … Sweeps5: sequential ≈ tens of minutes
LEAN_PATH=. lean BoundaryCensusMain.lean
LEAN_PATH=. lean BoundaryThmB.lean
```

To regenerate `BoundaryData.lean` from the ancillary engines: `python3 gen_data.py`
(reads `../anc/`; also re-runs the four normalization-pinning experiments for the
Jacobi layer and asserts them).

**Tier 2 (`BoundaryClaim`)** — the deduction layer. `ClaimFrom H m a` is the smallest
predicate closed under exactly the manuscript's inference rules, every side condition an
explicitly checked Bool. **Ten** cited rules —

Lefschetz (1,1) on X²_m · Aoki Thm 1-1 (𝔇-classes) · Aoki Thm 2-1 (standard, with its
gcd(x, m/p) = 1 hypothesis) · Aoki Thm 1-4(i) (join) · Aoki Thm 1-4(ii) (cancellation) ·
Theorem A (∗-split transport) · inflation · descent (`lem:infl` / `lem:descent`, Lemmas
4.1/5.2) · permutation invariance · Galois-orbit invariance of claim

— plus the uncited `hyp` (hypothesis introduction, for the relative derivations of the
walls), i.e. **eleven constructors, ten `Inputs` fields**; `hyp` is discharged separately by
`sound`'s `hH`.

`Inputs Alg` states those citations abstractly (one field per citation) and
`ClaimFrom.sound` proves every derivation transfers to any such `Alg`. The intended
instantiation is dimension-polymorphic and validity-guarded —

    Alg m a := "a is a Hodge character of grade g ≥ 1 at level m (so |a| = 2g) and
                V(a) ⊂ H^{|a|−2}(X_m^{|a|−2}) is spanned by algebraic cycle classes"

— because the rules genuinely change dimension: `lefschetz` speaks about H²(X²*m),
`standard` about X^{p−1}\_m, `join` about X⁶_m, X⁸_m, … (Aoki 1-4 changes n by r + s + 2).
The manuscript's own case is |a| = 6, g = 3, i.e. H⁴(X⁴_m); reading `Alg` as a \_fixed*
statement about H⁴(X⁴_m) would not satisfy the fields. Derived inside the calculus, hence
true for EVERY `Alg` satisfying the ten citations (in particular algebraicity — that
instantiation is the manuscript's geometric content, not this file's):

- `beyond_machinery` — the seven beyond-machinery orbits: w = (1,4,16,22,25,31) at 33 **via
  the level-66 lift** (join of the two Lefschetz quadruples through the (33,33) quasi-witness,
  cancellation, descent along x ↦ x²), the 45 and both 105 rows (σ₅,₁ standard blocks; the
  105-row-1 block routed through its level-35 inflation exactly as in the paper), and the
  induced 99/135/165 copies;
- `thmB_conditional` — **Theorem B at the census levels**: every grade-3 Hodge class of
  X⁴_m, m ∈ {9, 21, 27, 33, 39, 45}, satisfies Alg (the census sweep checker finds a
  derivation for every enumerated class);
- `starSplit_closures` — the Theorem-A closures at 39/117/195 (both orbits each);
- `walls` — the claim-equivalence walls as derivation transformers at Alg level:
  W₁₁₀ (110#1 ≃ 110#2 ≃ 220, through the 2-lift), W₁₁₄ (the triangle), W₇₀ (70 ≃ 210#2,
  through the 3-lift) — closing any member closes the wall.

**Tier 2 receipts (`BoundaryValidity`)** — the validity guards, proved rather than checked
(Lean core, no `native_decide`, standard axioms only). A faithfulness audit of the calculus
(2026-07-28) found that `pairsD` (Aoki Thm 1-1) accepted the empty class δ = [] — 𝔇^r_m has
r + 2 ≥ 2 entries, so there is no `V([])`, no `X^{-2}_m` and no cited Thm 1-1 instance —
and that `inflate`/`descend` accepted grade 0, a transport between varieties that do not
exist. `isDClassB` now carries a `2 ≤ length` fence and the three rules carry `1 ≤ g`; this
file proves the fences bite:

- `isDClassB_nil` / `pairsD_nil_blocked` — the audit's regression: Aoki 1-1 cannot
  introduce the empty class at any level or grade;
- `Claim.length_ge_two` — **calculus-wide**: EVERY class derivable in the absolute calculus
  has ≥ 2 entries, i.e. is a Hodge character of some grade g ≥ 1 living on an honest
  X_m^{2g−2} (induction over all eleven rules); hence `Claim.nil_not_derivable`.

The pre-existing derivations were unaffected — they use g ∈ {1, 2, 3} and δ of length 2 or
4 — so the guards cost nothing and every Tier-1/Tier-2 theorem still replays.

**Tier 1.5 (`BoundaryCensus` + `BoundaryCensusSweeps1–5` + `BoundaryCensusMain`)** — the FULL

- per-level sweeps (`native_decide`) at EVERY odd level 3 ≤ m ≤ 199 — all 99 of them, with no
  classical-level carve-out (the anc artifacts skip m < 21 and m = 23 as classical) — plus the
  three induced-risk levels 231/273/297: representative counts pinned against the anc
  three-engine receipts (`census_level_summaries.json`) resp. an independent Python enumeration

- `thmB_census_full` / `thmB_full_conditional` — THEOREM B's census leg at calculus level on

## Honest cut-lines (what is NOT formalised)

- **The geometry.** Lefschetz (1,1), Aoki's Theorems 1-1/1-4/2-1, the Shioda–Katsura
  transport behind Theorem A, and the finite-morphism inflation/descent are the _fields of
  the `Inputs` structure_ — cited, not proved. Instantiating them is exactly the
  manuscript's §§3–5; nothing here checks the geometric content.
- **Theorem B's legs are formalised at the DEDUCTION level only** (`BoundaryThmB`,
  2026-07-30; this closes the former "Theorem B beyond codimension 2" cut-line as far as a
  citation calculus can). `HC_of_census` machine-checks the manuscript's reduction — census
  closure + nine cited fields ⟹ every (c,c) Hodge class algebraic, c = 0..4 — but the nine
  fields (Lefschetz (1,1), hard Lefschetz, the rational primitive decomposition, Shioda's
  character-space spanning, …) are CITED, exactly like the ten `Inputs` fields. Instantiating
  them is the geometry, not this directory.
- **Theorem A is encoded in its odd-m form (unsynced drift, 2026-07-28).** The manuscript's
  current revision states `thm:A` for m ARBITRARY (its transport lemma `lem:transport`
  replaced the base point e = (1:−1:0), which forced m odd, by the rational zero-cycle
  z = (1/m)(C ∩ H)); the `starSplit` rule here still carries `m % 2 == 1`. The calculus is
  therefore strictly narrower than the printed theorem — sound, but not yet capturing its
  even-m half. The three certified ∗-split closures (39/117/195) are odd, so no result here
  depends on the gap. Re-syncing is a deliberate widening and waits for the revision round
  to close.
- **Census enumeration completeness: CLOSED** (`BoundaryCensus.census_enumeration_complete`,
  2026-07-30). Enumerator completeness is now a ∀-theorem at every level; the Tier-1 census
  theorems still quantify over the original `hodgeSextuples m` (nested loops), but the two
  enumerators are cross-checked element-for-element at the six original levels
  (`rawFull_matches_core`), and the full-range theorems quantify over `isHodgeB` itself.
  What remains trust-shaped here: the SOUNDNESS inclusion rawFull ⊆ {Hodge} rests on the
  exact half-unit verify in the join plus the per-level count pins (a junk element would
  make a pin or a closure sweep fail visibly), not on a formal theorem.
- **Scale: the ODD half is closed** (2026-07-30). The full odd census — all 99 odd levels
  3 ≤ m ≤ 199, including m = 23 and m < 21 which the anc artifacts skip as classical — and
  the probe levels 231/273/297 are replayed in Lean (`BoundaryCensusSweeps1–5`), with
  representative counts pinned to the anc three-engine receipts resp. an independent Python
  enumeration. Still NOT in Lean: the even-sector census to 250 with its deep partition
  certificates (the wall structure of the even sector remains certified at the certificate
  level — lattice verdicts + exchange edges — not by re-census).
- The `arXiv` tarball (`../boundary_note_arxiv.tar.gz`) is frozen and does NOT include this
  directory; whether to ship `lean/` with a revision is an editorial (user-gated) decision.
